## Dependencies and notices: exact distinction

Local installed versions were inspected alongside locks. Important examples: Next/React/openapi-typescript and Alembic declare MIT; Lucide combines ISC and applicable Feather MIT portions; psycopg3.3.6 declares LGPL-3.0-only; optional sharp/libvips platform entries carry LGPL/mixed expressions; axe-core/lightningcss entries carry MPL-2.0; caniuse-lite declares CC-BY-4.0. The JS lock includes 326 MIT, 26 MPL-2.0, 10 LGPL-only declarations and other/mixed expressions; these are metadata counts, not legal findings or proof that every optional binary ships.

For copied MIT/ISC portions retain their copyright and permission text. For Apache-covered copied material preserve license, applicable notices and modification notices; retain relevant upstream NOTICE material when present. [MPL2](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/) obligations concern covered files when distributed, including relevant source availability for executable distribution. [Psycopg's LGPL text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/psycopg/psycopg/master/LICENSE.txt) covers library/combined-work conditions: notices, source and modification/relinking obligations must be assessed for the actual distributed form. [CC-BY4](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) includes credit/license/change conditions. No conclusion is made that merely importing a dependency relicenses all project code, or that a future binary distribution is already compliant.

No upstream package sources, compiled chunks, model weights or wheel payloads are in the current source ZIP. Installing from a lock is separate from distributing copied source, browser bundles, wheels or Docker images. Existing source-level scaffolds and screenshots are the identifiable exceptions requiring notice/material treatment. Prepare correct notices from the **installed versions**, not only latest upstream main; no new project LICENSE/NOTICE grant was applied during this audit.

## Original-code license choices (D2)

| Choice | Practical grant/conditions for code you can license | Recommendation/limits |
|---|---|---|
| [MIT](https://opensource.org/license/mit) | Broad use/copy/modify/distribute/commercial permissions, preserving copyright/permission notice; warranty disclaimer. No comparable express patent-grant clause to Apache2 | Straightforward portfolio choice if you want permissive reuse and can license the original contributions. Does not cover others' data/models/code automatically |
| [Apache-2.0](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0) | Permissive copyright and express contributor patent provisions; relevant patent-claim termination, license/notice preservation and modified-file marking; no blanket trademark grant | Consider if explicit patent terms and contribution structure matter; more notice administration. Only licensable contributor rights can be granted |
| Public source-available with no open-source grant | Default copyright restrictions remain; does not confer general reuse/modification/distribution rights. GitHub's platform rights permit viewing/forking public repositories under its terms | Reasonable deliberate portfolio-only choice; do not label it open source. Public visibility is not permission to publish someone else's material |

[GitHub's licensing guidance](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/licensing-a-repository) distinguishes public visibility, open-source grants and platform viewing/forking rights. No obligation to adopt a specific license is asserted. D1 must identify ownership/employer/contributor/borrowed-code limits; AI service terms and applicable law may affect outputs. No AI-assisted ownership conclusion is made here.
